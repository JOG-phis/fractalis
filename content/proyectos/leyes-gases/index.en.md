What happens to the pressure of a gas when we compress it? And when we heat it? One container with a piston, a pressure gauge and a thermometer for discovering the three gas laws by measuring. The app is in Spanish.

## How to use it in class

- **Three laws, one lab:** each law only changes which quantity is kept fixed. In **Boyle's law** (fixed temperature) students move the piston; in **Charles's law** (fixed pressure) the piston carries a weight and rises by itself when heated; in **Gay-Lussac's law** (fixed volume) the piston is locked and the pressure rises instead.
- **The particles are visible:** they move faster at higher temperatures, and every collision with the walls leaves a flash. This shows what pressure is.
- **The law is always on screen:** the letters of the formula grow and shrink with each quantity, and the calculation below shows that the quantities change but the result (the constant) does not.
- **Virtual lab:** with «Anotar medida» (record a measurement) students fill in a table and see their points on the graph. The last column of the table is always the same: that is the law. «Mostrar la ley» (show the law) draws the hyperbola or the straight line, which for Charles and Gay-Lussac points to 0 K (−273 °C).
- It fits [2 ESO topic 2]({{< ref "2eso/apuntes/tema-2" >}}) and works as revision in 3 ESO. It can also replace or complement the [PhET activity]({{< ref "2eso/actividades/phet-gases" >}}).
