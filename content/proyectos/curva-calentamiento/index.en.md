Why doesn't the temperature of water rise while it boils? Heat ice until it turns into steam, or cool steam until it freezes, and watch what the particles do while the graph is drawn. The app is in Spanish.

## How to use it in class

- **Particles and graph at the same time:** in the solid the particles vibrate in an ordered lattice; when it melts they break free one by one; in the liquid they slide past each other at the bottom, and when it boils they escape from the surface. Meanwhile the temperature-time graph is drawn and a box explains what is happening in each stage.
- **Plateaus with their real length:** the model uses the specific and latent heats of each substance, so for water the boiling plateau lasts almost seven times longer than the melting one. A good question for class: why?
- **Comparing heating powers:** when the experiment is repeated with another power, the previous curve stays in grey. The plateaus are at the same temperature but last more or less time: the temperature of a change of state does not depend on how fast the substance is heated.
- **Heating and cooling:** «Enfriar» (cool) gives the cooling curve, with condensation and freezing.
- **Mystery substance:** students read the temperature of the two plateaus on the graph and look it up in a table of melting and boiling points, which shows that these are characteristic properties.
- It fits the heating and cooling graphs section of [2 ESO topic 2]({{< ref "2eso/apuntes/tema-2" >}}) and works as revision in 3 ESO.
