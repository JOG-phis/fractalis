### Two exercises to get started

{{< ejercicio n="3" pista="The solvent is the component present in the larger proportion. To tell a colloid from a solution, think of a torch." solucion="a) Solute: the sugar. Solvent: the water. b) No: milk is a colloid. They can be told apart by the **Tyndall effect**: the beam of a torch is visible in the tea with milk, but not in the tea with sugar." >}}
Tea with sugar is a solution. a) State which is the solute and which is the solvent. b) If we add milk, is it still a solution? Name a test that lets you tell the two types of mixture apart.
{{< /ejercicio >}}

{{< ejercicio n="5" pista="Start with what you can remove without water. Salt dissolves in water; sand does not." solucion="1st **Magnetic separation:** the magnet attracts the nails. 2nd We add water and **filter**: the sand stays in the filter. 3rd We **evaporate** the water from the filtrate: the salt is left." >}}
If sand, salt and nails are mixed together, which separation techniques would you use and in what order?
{{< /ejercicio >}}
