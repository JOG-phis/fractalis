### Two exercises to get started

{{< ejercicio n="2" pista="Rearrange \(c = \dfrac{\text{mass of solute}}{\text{volume}}\) for the mass of solute and convert the volume to litres." solucion="mass of solute \(= c \cdot V = 40\,\text{g/L} \cdot 0.15\,\text{L} = 6\,\text{g}\) of sodium chloride." >}}
We make a solution of sodium chloride in water with a concentration of 40 g/L. If we take 150 mL of this solution, how much sodium chloride will it contain?
{{< /ejercicio >}}

{{< ejercicio n="5" pista="mass of solution = mass of solute + mass of solvent." solucion="a) A: \(\dfrac{8}{100} \cdot 100 = 8\,\%\). B: \(\dfrac{15}{150} \cdot 100 = 10\,\%\). b) **B** is more concentrated: it has more salt in every 100 g of solution." >}}
We compare two solutions of salt in water. **A:** 8 g of salt with 92 g of water. **B:** 15 g of salt with 135 g of water.
a) Calculate the concentration of both as a percentage by mass. b) Which one is more concentrated?
{{< /ejercicio >}}
