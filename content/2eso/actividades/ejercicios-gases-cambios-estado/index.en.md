Problems on the **gas laws** (Boyle's, Charles's and Gay-Lussac's) and on **change-of-state graphs**. Students answer on the sheet itself.
{{< /lead >}}

{{< caja nota "How to solve them:" >}}
write down the data with their units, convert the temperature to **kelvin**, write the formula and the name of the law, rearrange it, substitute with units and give the result with its unit.
{{< /caja >}}

### Two exercises to get started

{{< ejercicio n="1" pista="The temperature does not change: it is **Boyle's law**, \(P_1 \cdot V_1 = P_2 \cdot V_2\)." solucion="\(V_2 = \dfrac{P_1 \cdot V_1}{P_2} = \dfrac{2\,\text{atm} \cdot 50\,\text{L}}{4\,\text{atm}} = 25\,\text{L}\)" >}}
A gas at constant temperature takes up a volume of 50 L at a pressure of 2 atm. What volume will it take up if we double the pressure?
{{< /ejercicio >}}

{{< ejercicio n="3" pista="The volume does not change: it is **Gay-Lussac's law**. Convert the temperatures to kelvin." solucion="\(T_1 = 288\,\text{K}\), \(T_2 = 318\,\text{K}\). \(P_2 = \dfrac{P_1 \cdot T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{2\,\text{atm} \cdot 318\,\text{K}}{288\,\text{K}} = 2.21\,\text{atm}\)" >}}
Inside a car's tyres, the air is at 15 °C and 2 atm. Because of friction with the road, the temperature rises to 45 °C. If the volume of the tyre does not change, what pressure will that air exert?
{{< /ejercicio >}}
