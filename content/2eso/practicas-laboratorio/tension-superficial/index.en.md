{{< lead >}}
Surface tension is the "elastic skin" that forms on the surface of a liquid. In this homemade practical we'll test it, coin by coin and drop by drop, by applying the scientific method step by step.
{{< /lead >}}

## 1. Objectives

1. Apply the steps of the **scientific method** in an orderly way.
2. Write a **lab report** based on a real experience.

## 2. Introduction

The surface tension of a liquid is a physical quantity related to the cohesive forces between its molecules, especially the ones at the surface. The combined action of all of them creates a resistance to breaking the liquid's free surface, as if it were a thin elastic film. As you'll verify, this resistance depends on several factors.

{{< alert icon="wand-magic-sparkles" >}}
**Did you know...?** Thanks to surface tension, some insects, like the water strider, can walk on water without sinking. For the same reason, if you fill a glass very carefully, you can get the water level to rise a few millimetres above the rim without spilling.
{{< /alert >}}

## 3. Materials

- Coins (at least two different ones and two identical ones)
- Water
- Dropper
- Kitchen paper
- Soap (dish soap works best)

## 4. Experimental method

### Part 1 — Does the coin's size matter?

1. Place two different coins on a piece of kitchen paper.
2. Using the dropper, add water drop by drop to each coin and count how many drops fit before the water spills over.
3. Repeat the process 3 times per coin.
4. Record your results in a table like this one:

### Part 2 — Does soap matter?

1. Place two **identical** coins on a piece of kitchen paper.
2. With your finger, add a tiny amount of soap to the surface of one of them.
3. Repeat the drop-by-drop count from Part 1 on both coins.
4. Repeat the process 3 times.
5. Record your results:

{{< alert icon="triangle-exclamation" cardColor="#dc2626" iconColor="#ffffff" textColor="#fef2f2" >}}
**Careful with the soap:** one drop too many "contaminates" the experiment and skews the result on the next coins. Use tiny amounts and wash and dry your hands well between coins.
{{< /alert >}}

## 5. Discussion

1. How many drops fit on each coin before it spills over? What conclusion do you draw from your first table?
2. How does the presence of soap affect the surface tension of water? Write your conclusion as clearly as possible based on the data in the second table.

## 6. Going further

Choose a coin and let drops of fridge-cold water fall until it spills over. Clean and dry the coin and repeat the experiment, this time with water warmed in the microwave by a responsible adult.
