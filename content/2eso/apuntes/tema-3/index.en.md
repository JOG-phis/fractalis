## 1. Material systems

{{< figura src="clasificacion-en.svg" alt="Diagram: material systems are divided into pure substances (elements and compounds) and mixtures (heterogeneous, colloids and homogeneous or solutions)" pie="Classification of material systems." ancho="620" >}}

{{< columnas >}}
{{< caja idea "Pure substance:" >}}
made up of **a single substance**. It has a fixed composition and specific properties.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja idea "Mixture:" >}}
made up of **two or more substances** that keep their properties. Their proportions can vary.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /columnas >}}

## 2. Elements and compounds

- **Element:** a pure substance that **cannot be broken down** into simpler ones. It is represented by a symbol: Fe, O, Au.
- **Compound:** a pure substance made up of **two or more elements** in a fixed proportion, with new properties. It is represented by a formula: \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(\mathrm{NaCl}\).

## 3. Mixtures

{{< figura src="tres-mezclas-en.svg" alt="Three beakers: a heterogeneous mixture (water and oil), a colloid (milk) and a solution (salt water)" ancho="420" >}}

| Type | Can you tell the components apart? | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Heterogeneous** | Yes, with the naked eye or a magnifying glass. | water and oil, granite |
| **Colloid** | Only with a microscope. | milk, mayonnaise, fog |
| **Solution** | No, not even with a microscope. | salt water, air, bronze |

{{< figtexto lado="derecha" src="tyndall-en.svg" alt="A beam of light passes through a solution without being seen and becomes visible as it passes through a colloid" ancho="340" >}}
{{< caja nota "Tyndall effect:" >}}
when it passes through a **colloid**, the light beam **is visible**; in a **solution**, it is not. This is how we tell a colloid from a solution.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /figtexto >}}

## 4. Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture:

- **Solvent:** the component present in the **larger** proportion. The solution has the same state as its solvent.
- **Solute:** the component present in the **smaller** proportion.

{{< figtexto lado="derecha" src="diluida-en.svg" alt="Two beakers: a dilute solution with few solute particles and a concentrated solution with many" ancho="260" >}}
A solution is **dilute** if it has little solute and **concentrated** if it has a lot.
{{< /figtexto >}}

## 5. The concentration of a solution

| Form | Expression | Meaning |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Mass concentration** | \({c = \dfrac{\text{mass of solute (g)}}{\text{volume of solution (L)}}}\) | g of solute in 1 L of solution |
| **Percentage by mass** | \(\dfrac{\text{mass of solute}}{\text{mass of solution}} \cdot 100\) | g of solute in 100 g of solution |
| **Percentage by volume** | \(\dfrac{\text{volume of solute}}{\text{volume of solution}} \cdot 100\) | mL of solute in 100 mL of solution |

{{< formula >}}\text{mass of solution} = \text{mass of solute} + \text{mass of solvent}{{< /formula >}}
