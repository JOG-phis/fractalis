## 1. The kinetic-molecular theory

It is the model that explains what matter is made of and why solids, liquids and gases are different. It is based on four **postulates**:

{{< columnas >}}
{{< postulado n="1" icono="postulado-1.svg" alt="A particle surrounded by six others" >}}Matter is made up of very small **particles**.{{< /postulado >}}
{{< postulado n="2" icono="postulado-2-en.svg" alt="A cold particle with a short arrow and a hot particle with a long arrow" >}}Particles are in **constant motion**: the higher the temperature, the faster they move.{{< /postulado >}}
{{< postulado n="3" icono="postulado-3-en.svg" alt="Two particles separated by empty space" >}}There is **empty space** between the particles.{{< /postulado >}}
{{< postulado n="4" icono="postulado-4.svg" alt="Two particles attracting each other" >}}There are **forces of attraction** between the particles.{{< /postulado >}}
{{< /columnas >}}

## 2. The states of matter

{{< figura src="estado-solido.svg|estado-liquido.svg|estado-gas.svg" alt="Solid: particles very close together and ordered|Liquid: particles close together but disordered|Gas: particles far apart, moving in all directions" ancho="140|140|140" pie="Solid, liquid and gas." >}}

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Shape** | fixed | variable | variable |
| **Volume** | fixed | fixed | variable |
| **Can it be compressed?** | no | hardly at all | yes |
| **Does it expand?** | no | no | yes |
| **Particles** | very close together and ordered; they only vibrate | close together and disordered; they slide past each other | far apart; they move freely |

## 3. Changes of state

{{< caja idea "Change of state:" >}}
when a substance goes from one state to another. The substance **stays the same**: it is a **physical change**.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="cambios-estado-en.svg" alt="Diagram with the three states at the corners of a triangle and red (progressive) and blue (regressive) arrows for the six changes of state" pie="The changes of state." ancho="580" >}}

- <span class="progresivo">Progressive changes</span> (the substance **absorbs** energy): melting, vaporisation and sublimation.
- <span class="regresivo">Regressive changes</span> (the substance **releases** energy): freezing, condensation and deposition.

Vaporisation can happen by **evaporation** (only at the surface, at any temperature) or by **boiling** (throughout the liquid, at a fixed temperature).

{{< caja nota "Remember:" >}}
the **melting point** is the same as the **freezing point**, and the **boiling point** is the same as the **condensation point**. They are **characteristic properties**: they help us identify a substance. Water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.
{{< /caja >}}

## 4. Heating and cooling curves

{{< figura src="grafica-calentamiento-en.svg" alt="Temperature-time graph of water: it rises from −20 to 0 °C, stays flat at 0 °C, rises to 100 °C, stays flat at 100 °C and rises to 120 °C" pie="Heating curve of water." ancho="600" >}}

- **Sloping section:** only one state; the temperature changes.
- **Flat section:** a change of state; there are two states at the same time and the **temperature does not change**.

## 5. Pressure, volume and temperature of a gas

- **Pressure:** caused by the particles hitting the walls of the container. \(1\,\text{atm} = 760\,\text{mmHg} = 101\,325\,\text{Pa}\)
- **Volume:** the volume of the container. \(1\,\text{L} = 1000\,\text{mL} = 1\,\text{dm}^3\)
- **Temperature:** tells us how fast the particles are moving.

{{< formula >}}T\,(\text{K}) = T\,(^\circ\text{C}) + 273{{< /formula >}}

{{< caja cuidado "Careful:" >}}
in the gas laws, temperature must **always be in kelvin**.
{{< /caja >}}

## 6. The gas laws

In all of them the amount of gas does not change and one of the three quantities stays constant.

| Law | Constant | Relationship | Equation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| **Boyle's law** | \(T\) | \(P\) and \(V\) are inversely proportional | \(P_1 \cdot V_1 = P_2 \cdot V_2\) |
| **Charles's law** | \(P\) | \(V\) and \(T\) are directly proportional | \(\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}\) |
| **Gay-Lussac's law** | \(V\) | \(P\) and \(T\) are directly proportional | \(\dfrac{P_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2}{T_2}\) |
