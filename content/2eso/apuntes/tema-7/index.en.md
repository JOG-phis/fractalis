## 1. Energy

{{< caja idea "Energy:" >}}
the ability of bodies to **produce changes**.
{{< /caja >}}

- It is **transformed**, **transferred**, **stored** and **transported**.
- It is **conserved**: it is neither created nor destroyed, only transformed.
- It is **degraded**: in every transformation, part of it becomes heat that cannot be used.

### Units of energy

The International System unit is the **joule** (J).

| Equivalences |
| :---: |
| \(1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}\) |
| \(1\,\text{cal} = 4.18\,\text{J}\) |
| \(1\,\text{kcal} = 1000\,\text{cal}\) |
| \(1\,\text{kWh} = 3\,600\,000\,\text{J}\) |

To change units we use **conversion factors**:

{{< formula >}}2\,\text{kcal} \cdot \frac{1000\,\text{cal}}{1\,\text{kcal}} \cdot \frac{4.18\,\text{J}}{1\,\text{cal}} = 8360\,\text{J}{{< /formula >}}

| | Which they are | Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-renewable** | coal, oil and natural gas (fossil fuels) and uranium (nuclear) | They run out. They pollute (\(\mathrm{CO_2}\), radioactive waste). Always available. |

## 3. Temperature and heat

{{< caja idea "Temperature:" >}}
tells us how hot or cold a body is: the faster its particles move, the higher it is. It is measured with a **thermometer**.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="temperatura-en.svg" alt="Particles moving slowly at low temperature and fast at high temperature" ancho="460" >}}

- Scales: **Celsius** (°C) and **Kelvin** (K, the International System one).
- **Absolute zero:** 0 K = −273 °C. There are no negative temperatures in kelvin.

{{< formula >}}T\,(\text{K}) = T\,(^\circ\text{C}) + 273{{< /formula >}}

{{< caja idea "Heat:" >}}
the energy that passes from one body to another at a **different temperature**, always from the hotter one to the colder one. It is measured in J (or in cal).
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="equilibrio-termico-en.svg" alt="A body at 80 °C and another at 20 °C in contact both end up at 50 °C" pie="In the end, both bodies reach the same temperature: **thermal equilibrium**." ancho="460" >}}

{{< caja cuidado >}}
bodies **do not have** heat: they have a temperature and they **give out** or **absorb** heat.
{{< /caja >}}

## 4. The effects of heat

| Effect | What happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Change of temperature** | When it absorbs heat, a body warms up; when it gives it out, it cools down. It depends on the mass and the material. |
| **Thermal expansion** | When heated, bodies get bigger; when cooled, they contract. Gases expand the most. |
| **Change of state** | Progressive (they absorb heat): melting, vaporisation and sublimation. Regressive (they give out heat): freezing, condensation and deposition. During the change, the temperature stays the same. |
