## 1. Physics and chemistry

They are two sciences that study **matter**, **energy** and the **changes** that take place in nature.

{{< columnas >}}
{{< caja idea "Physics:" >}}
studies phenomena in which the **nature** of substances **does not change**: motion, forces, light, sound… *Example:* water freezing.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja idea "Chemistry:" >}}
studies the **composition** of matter and the changes in which some substances **turn into others**. *Example:* a piece of paper burning.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /columnas >}}

## 2. The scientific method

{{< caja idea "Scientific method:" >}}
the set of ordered steps that scientists follow to investigate nature and obtain reliable knowledge.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="metodo-en.svg" alt="The five stages of the scientific method in order: observation, hypothesis, experimentation, analysis of results and communication. If the hypothesis is not confirmed, a new one is proposed" ancho="640" >}}

1. **Observation:** we notice a phenomenon and ask ourselves a question.
2. **Hypothesis:** we propose an explanation that can be tested.
3. **Experimentation:** we carry out experiments and record the data.
4. **Analysis of results:** we organise the data in tables and graphs and draw conclusions.
5. **Communication:** we make the results known.

## 3. Matter and its properties

{{< caja idea "Matter:" >}}
everything that has **mass** and takes up space, that is, has **volume**.
{{< /caja >}}

| Properties can be… | | |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General or specific** | **General:** all matter has them and they cannot be used to identify a substance (mass, volume, temperature). | **Specific:** each substance has its own value, so they can be used to identify it (density, melting point). |
| **Qualitative or quantitative** | **Qualitative:** they cannot be measured; they are described with words (colour, smell, shine). | **Quantitative:** they can be measured; they are expressed with a number and a unit (mass, density). |
| **Extensive or intensive** | **Extensive:** they depend on the amount of matter (mass, volume). | **Intensive:** they do not depend on the amount of matter (temperature, density, colour). |

## 4. Physical quantities and units

{{< caja idea "Physical quantity:" >}}
any property of matter that can be **measured**. To **measure** is to compare it with another one of the same kind that is taken as the **unit**: the result is a number and its unit (3 m, 25 kg, 40 s).
{{< /caja >}}

| Quantity | Unit | Symbol |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Length** | metre | m |
| **Mass** | kilogram | kg |
| **Time** | second | s |
| **Capacity** | litre | L |

{{< caja nota "Remember:" >}}
symbols are written **without a full stop** and **have no plural**: 5 kg (not "5 kgs."). The litre is not an International System unit, but it is the most widely used one for capacity; 1 L = 1 dm³.
{{< /caja >}}

## 5. Multiples and submultiples: the staircase

{{< figura src="escalera-en.svg" alt="Staircase of prefixes: kilo, hecto, deca, unit, deci, centi and milli. Going down, multiply by 10 at each step; going up, divide by 10" ancho="640" >}}

- Going **down** one step: multiply by 10 (add a 0 or move the decimal point to the right).
- Going **up** one step: divide by 10 (remove a 0 or move the decimal point to the left).

*Example:* \(3\,\text{km} \rightarrow \text{m}\): go down 3 steps, \(3 \times 1000 =\) {{< resultado >}}3000 m{{< /resultado >}}

## 6. Scientific notation

{{< caja idea "Scientific notation:" >}}
writing a number as the product of a number with **a single whole-number digit** (other than zero) and a **power of 10**, for example \(4.5 \times 10^{6}\).
{{< /caja >}}

| Number | Scientific notation | |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| 45 000 | \(4.5 \times 10^{4}\) | the decimal point moves 4 places to the left |
| 0.0062 | \(6.2 \times 10^{-3}\) | the decimal point moves 3 places to the right |

If the number is **large**, the exponent is **positive**; if it is **small** (it starts with 0), the exponent is **negative**.

## 7. Conversion factors

{{< caja idea "Conversion factor:" >}}
a fraction with the **same value** on top and on the bottom, but in different units. It is worth 1: multiplying by it does not change the quantity, only the unit. The unit we want to **remove** goes on the **bottom**.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< formula >}}3.5\,\textcolor{#ef4444}{\cancel{\text{km}}} \times \frac{1000\,\text{m}}{1\,\textcolor{#ef4444}{\cancel{\text{km}}}} = 3500\,\text{m}{{< /formula >}}

## 8. The laboratory

### Lab rules

1. Wear gloves, a lab coat and goggles.
2. Don't eat or drink.
3. Don't sniff or taste anything.
4. Don't mix anything without reading the label.
5. No open shoes.
6. Tie your hair back.

### Hazard pictograms

Hazardous chemicals carry red diamonds with a black symbol on their label, warning of the type of hazard.
