## Who is behind this

I teach Physics and Chemistry at a state secondary school in Andalusia, Spain. I come from physics: I hold a degree in Physics and a master's in Physics and Nanotechnology. I teach ESO and Bachillerato and, since this school year, Computing and Robotics in 1 ESO too.

I am interested in almost anything to do with learning and teaching science: active learning (breakouts, cooperative work, inquiry), assessment, programming and robotics, 3D printing and, more and more, artificial intelligence in education.

## Teaching, I learn

Goya drew this old man with two walking sticks when he was nearly eighty, and wrote above him: «Aún aprendo», "I am still learning". It is the idea that best sums up how I experience the classroom. Preparing a lesson forces you to really understand things, and every group teaches you something: a question you did not expect, an activity that works better than planned, or one that has to be redone from scratch. As Seneca put it, while we teach, we learn.

Fractalis is, at heart, my learning notebook: this is where I keep what I am learning about teaching. And for the days when you need a push, I keep a small collection of [quotes to keep us going]({{< ref "citas" >}}).

## Knowledge that belongs to everyone

I make these words by the philosopher of science Paul Feyerabend my own:

{{< cita ancho="completo" autor="Paul Feyerabend" fuente="*Farewell to Reason*, preface to the Spanish edition, “Knowledge for survival” (translated from the Spanish)" >}}
… let us develop a new kind of knowledge that is human, not because it embodies an abstract idea of humanity, but because everyone can take part in building and changing it, and let us use this knowledge to solve the two problems still facing us today, the problem of survival and the problem of peace: on the one hand, peace among human beings and, on the other, peace between human beings and the whole of Nature.
{{< /cita >}}

Sharing my materials openly is my small way of building that knowledge together.

## Why I share

When I started teaching, other teachers' websites got me through more than one school year (you will find them in [Friendly sites]({{< ref "paginas-amigas" >}})). Fractalis is my way of returning the favour.

## What you will find

- **Notes** for each unit: a summary of the essentials on the web and the full unit as a PDF.
- **Activities**: exercise sheets, breakouts, readings, simulations, lab practicals and tasks. Each one shows how many lessons it takes, how students are grouped and what materials you need.
- **Projects**: Computing and Robotics, programming, AI, 3D printing and other resources that do not belong to a particular year.
- Everything is based on the **official Andalusian curriculum**. If it helps, here are the [Physics and Chemistry curriculum tables]({{< ref "curriculo" >}}): specific competences, assessment criteria and core knowledge for each year.
- The pages are in **Spanish and English**; the PDFs, for now, only in Spanish.

## Use it, adapt it, share it

All the materials are licensed under [Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). You can download them, adapt them to your group and share them, as long as you:

- credit the source (for example: "Fractalis — Physics and Chemistry at every scale", with a link to the resource);
- do not use them for commercial purposes;
- share whatever you make from them under the same licence.

## How it is made

Fractalis is a static website built with [Hugo](https://gohugo.io/) and the [Blowfish](https://blowfish.page/) theme, and published on GitHub Pages. It has no advertising and no visitor analytics. The fractal for each year is generated with code.
