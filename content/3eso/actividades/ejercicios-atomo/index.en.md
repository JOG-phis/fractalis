### Two exercises to get started

{{< ejercicio n="1" pista="Weighted average: mass \(\times\) abundance (as a decimal) for each isotope, then add." solucion="\(63 \cdot 0.6909 + 65 \cdot 0.3091 \approx 63.62\ \text{u}\)." >}}
**Atomic mass of copper.** Copper occurs in nature as two isotopes with masses of 63 u and 65 u. Their relative abundances are 69.09 % and 30.91 %, respectively. Calculate the atomic mass of copper.
{{< /ejercicio >}}

{{< ejercicio n="3" pista="With two isotopes, what do their abundances add up to?" solucion="B-11 has an abundance of 80.1 %; the atomic mass is \(\approx 10.81\ \text{u}\)." >}}
**Atomic mass of boron.** Boron has two natural isotopes: B-10, with a mass of 10.013 u and a relative abundance of 19.9 %, and B-11, with a mass of 11.009 u. Calculate the atomic mass of boron.
{{< /ejercicio >}}
