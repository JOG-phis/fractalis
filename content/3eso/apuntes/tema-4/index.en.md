## 1. Physical changes and chemical changes

{{< columnas >}}
{{< caja idea "Physical change:" >}}
the substances **stay the same**: changes of state, dissolving…
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja idea "Chemical reaction:" >}}
the **reactants** turn into **products**, new substances with different properties.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /columnas >}}

**Signs** that a reaction has taken place: a change of colour, a gas given off, a precipitate forming, a change of temperature, light given off or a new smell.

{{< figura src="fisico-quimico-en.png" alt="Physical change: ice melts and the water molecules do not change. Chemical change: hydrogen and oxygen form water" ancho="560" >}}

## 2. Inside a reaction

In a reaction the **bonds** in the reactants **break** and the atoms join up in a different way to give the products: atoms are **neither created nor destroyed**.

{{< caja idea "Collision theory:" >}}
for a reaction to happen, the particles must **collide** with enough energy and the right orientation (**effective collisions**).
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="colisiones-en.png" alt="Effective collision: the molecules collide with the right energy and orientation, form an activated complex and give the products. Ineffective collision: they bounce off without reacting" ancho="420" >}}

## 3. The chemical equation and balancing it

{{< figura src="partes-en.svg" alt="Parts of the equation 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O: reactants on the left, products on the right; the coefficient 2 means two molecules of H2 and the subscript 2 means two H atoms in each molecule" ancho="520" >}}

{{< caja idea "Balancing an equation:" >}}
putting the smallest whole-number **coefficients** in front so that there is the **same number of atoms** of each element on both sides of the arrow.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< caja cuidado "Watch out! Never change the subscripts." >}}
you only put numbers **in front of** the formulae: \(\ce{H2O}\) is water, but \(\ce{H2O2}\) is hydrogen peroxide.
{{< /caja >}}

**Trial and error**, always in this order: first the **metals**, then the **non-metals** other than H and O, then **hydrogen** and finally **oxygen**. If you get a decimal coefficient, multiply everything by 2. At the end, **check**.

{{< formula >}}\ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}{{< /formula >}}

{{< figura src="ajuste-agua-en.png" alt="In the balanced equation 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O there are 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms in the reactants and the same in the products" ancho="380" >}}

## 4. The laws of chemical reactions

{{< figtexto lado="derecha" src="lavoisier-en.svg" alt="A closed flask reads 150.0 g on the balance before and after the reaction" ancho="300" >}}
{{< caja idea "Lavoisier's law:" >}}
in a chemical reaction, the mass of the reactants equals the mass of the products (in a closed container).
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja idea "Proust's law:" >}}
elements always combine in a **fixed mass ratio** to form a compound.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /figtexto >}}

Both laws are explained by the fact that atoms are conserved and each compound has a fixed formula.

## 5. The mole and calculations with reactions

{{< caja idea "Mole:" >}}
the unit of amount of substance: 1 mol contains \(6.022 \cdot 10^{23}\) particles (Avogadro's number, \(N_A\)). The **molar mass** \(M\) (g/mol) is the mass of one mole and has the same value as the molecular mass.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< formula >}}n = \frac{m}{M} \qquad\qquad N = n \cdot N_A{{< /formula >}}

The coefficients of a balanced equation stand for **moles**: \(\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}\) reads «2 mol of hydrogen react with 1 mol of oxygen to form 2 mol of water».

{{< figura src="camino-masa-en.svg" alt="Stoichiometric calculation: the mass of A divided by its molar mass gives the moles of A; the coefficients of the equation give the moles of B; multiplied by its molar mass they give the mass of B" pie="A stoichiometric calculation always goes from mass to moles, from moles of A to moles of B, and from moles to mass." ancho="560" >}}

{{< caja ejemplo "mass of water formed" >}}
What mass of water is formed from 8 g of \(\ce{H2}\)? \(\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}\)

{{< formula >}}m_{\ce{H2O}} = 8\,\text{g } \ce{H2} \cdot \frac{1\,\text{mol } \ce{H2}}{2\,\text{g } \ce{H2}} \cdot \frac{2\,\text{mol } \ce{H2O}}{2\,\text{mol } \ce{H2}} \cdot \frac{18\,\text{g } \ce{H2O}}{1\,\text{mol } \ce{H2O}} = 72\,\text{g } \ce{H2O}{{< /formula >}}
{{< /caja >}}

## 6. Energy and rate of reaction

{{< caja idea "Activation energy (Ea):" >}}
the minimum energy the particles need for a collision to be effective.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="entalpia-en.svg" alt="Energy diagrams: in an exothermic reaction the products have less energy than the reactants and energy is released; in an endothermic one they have more and energy is absorbed" ancho="600" >}}

A reaction is **exothermic** if it gives out energy (combustion) and **endothermic** if it takes it in.

| Factor | The reaction is faster if… | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temperature** | the temperature goes up | the fridge keeps food fresh |
| **Concentration** | there are more reactant particles | strong vinegar and limestone |
| **Surface area** | the solid is in small pieces or powdered | a crushed effervescent tablet |
| **Catalyst** | a catalyst is added, which lowers \(E_a\) | enzymes, a car's catalytic converter |

## 8. Chemistry, the environment and society

- **Greenhouse effect:** burning fossil fuels produces \(\ce{CO2}\), which traps part of the Earth's heat in the atmosphere. As it increases, the average temperature of the planet rises: this is **climate change**.
- **Acid rain:** burning fuels that contain sulfur forms sulfur oxides, which react with the water in clouds to form sulfuric acid: \(\ce{SO3 + H2O -> H2SO4}\).
- **Serving society:** fertilisers (synthesis of ammonia), medicines such as antacids (neutralisation) and cars' catalytic converters, which turn toxic gases into less harmful ones.
