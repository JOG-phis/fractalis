## 1. Introduction

Depending on the number of elements, compounds are **binary** (2 elements: hydrides, oxides, peroxides and binary salts), **ternary** (3: hydroxides, oxoacids, oxysalts) or **quaternary** (4: acid salts). This topic covers binary compounds and hydroxides.

{{< caja idea "Writing order:" >}}
in the **formula**, the **electropositive** part goes first \((\ce{NaCl})\). In English the **name** follows the same order (*sodium chloride*); in Spanish it is the other way round (*cloruro de sodio*).
{{< /caja >}}

{{< caja idea "Electronegativity:" >}}
the tendency of an atom to attract the shared electrons in a bond. It increases towards the top and the right of the periodic table (fluorine is the most electronegative).
{{< /caja >}}

{{< figura src="secuencia.svg" alt="IUPAC electronegativity sequence, which runs through the periodic table group by group up to fluorine" pie="IUPAC sequence: the element that comes later is taken as the more electronegative one." ancho="620" >}}

## 2. Two systems of nomenclature

- **Compositional (multiplying prefixes):** shows how many atoms of each element there are with prefixes: mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-, hexa-, hepta-… «Mono-» is almost always left out.
- **Charge numbers:** shows the charge of the metal in brackets, **with no space**: iron(3+). If the metal has only one oxidation number, it is not written; and it is not used with non-metals.

### Hydrides

- **Metal hydrides** (metal + hydrogen): hydrogen has \(-1\). \(\ce{PbH4}\): <span class="fe">lead</span> <span class="fp">tetra</span>hydride or <span class="fe">lead</span><span class="fp">(4+)</span> hydride.
- **Groups 16 and 17**: hydrogen goes on the **left** (\(\ce{HCl}\), \(\ce{H2S}\)) and no prefixes are used. Dissolved in water they are **hydracids**: \(\ce{HCl(aq)}\) <span class="fs">hydro</span><span class="fe">chlor</span><span class="fs">ic</span> <span class="fp">acid</span>. Water is the exception: \(\ce{H2O}\) is hydrogen oxide.
- **Groups 13, 14 and 15**: hydrogen goes on the **right** by convention (\(\ce{NH3}\), \(\ce{CH4}\)). They have their own names: methane \((\ce{CH4})\), silane \((\ce{SiH4})\), phosphane \((\ce{PH3})\), **ammonia** \((\ce{NH3})\).

### Oxides and peroxides

- **Oxides:** oxygen has \(-2\). To write the formula from the oxidation numbers, **swap** them as subscripts and simplify if you can: \(\ce{Pb^{4+}}\) and \(\ce{O^{2-}}\) → \(\ce{Pb2O4}\) → \(\ce{PbO2}\).
- **Peroxides:** they contain the \(\ce{O2^{2-}}\) group, which is **never simplified**: \(\ce{H2O2}\), hydrogen peroxide.

{{< caja cuidado >}}
the halogens are the exception to the order with oxygen: \(\ce{Cl2O7}\) is <span class="fp">di</span><span class="fe">chlorine</span> <span class="fp">hepta</span>oxide, but \(\ce{OF2}\) is oxygen <span class="fp">di</span><span class="fe">fluor</span><span class="fs">ide</span>, because fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen.
{{< /caja >}}

### Binary salts and hydroxides

- **Binary salts** (metal + non-metal, or two non-metals): the more electronegative non-metal ends in **-ide**. \(\ce{FeCl3}\): <span class="fb">iron</span> <span class="fp">tri</span><span class="fe">chlor</span><span class="fs">ide</span> or <span class="fb">iron</span><span class="fp">(3+)</span> <span class="fe">chlor</span><span class="fs">ide</span>.
- **Hydroxides:** a metal cation and the hydroxide anion \(\ce{OH-}\), which goes in brackets if there is more than one: \(\ce{Al(OH)3}\), aluminium hydroxide.

## 5. Oxidation numbers and charge numbers

{{< columnas >}}
{{< caja nota "Oxidation number:" >}}
the electrons an atom loses or gains when it forms a compound. In ionic compounds it is the same as the charge of the ion; in molecules of a single element (\(\ce{Cl2}\), \(\ce{O2}\)) it is **zero**.
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja nota "Charge number:" >}}
the net charge of an **ion**. It only makes sense for ions and ionic compounds: \(\ce{OH-}\) has a charge of 1−.
{{< /caja >}}
