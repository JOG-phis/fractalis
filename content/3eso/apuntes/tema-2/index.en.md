## 1. Elements and compounds

{{< caja idea "Chemical element:" >}}
a type of atom defined by its atomic number \(Z\) (its number of protons). It is represented by a **symbol**: the first letter is a capital and the second, if there is one, is lower case (Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide).
{{< /caja >}}

{{< columnas >}}
{{< caja nota "Simple substance:" >}}
made of **a single element**: \(\ce{Fe}\), \(\ce{O2}\), \(\ce{He}\).
{{< /caja >}}
{{< caja nota "Compound:" >}}
made of **two or more elements** in a fixed proportion: \(\ce{H2O}\), \(\ce{NaCl}\), \(\ce{CO2}\).
{{< /caja >}}
{{< /columnas >}}

Atoms can be **isolated** (noble gases), form **molecules** (small groups of bonded atoms, such as \(\ce{O2}\) or \(\ce{H2O}\)) or form **crystals** (huge, ordered lattices, such as salt or metals).

## 2. The periodic table

The elements are arranged by atomic number in **7 periods** (rows) and **18 groups** (columns). Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties.

{{< figura src="tabla-familias-en.svg" alt="Periodic table showing the families: alkali metals (group 1), alkaline earth metals (2), transition metals (3 to 12), boron group (13), carbon group (14), nitrogen group (15), oxygen group (16), halogens (17) and noble gases (18)" pie="The families of the periodic table." ancho="620" >}}

{{< figura src="tabla-tipos-en.svg" alt="Coloured periodic table: metals on the left and in the centre, non-metals at the top right, metalloids between them and noble gases in the last column" pie="Metals, metalloids, non-metals and noble gases." ancho="560" >}}

{{< caja idea "Valence electrons:" >}}
the electrons in the **outer shell** of the atom. They are the ones involved when atoms bond. In groups 1 and 2 they match the group number; in groups 13 to 18, its last digit (group 15 → 5). Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons.
{{< /caja >}}

## 3. The octet rule

{{< caja idea "Octet rule:" >}}
atoms tend to **gain, lose or share** electrons until they have **8 in their outer shell**, like the nearest noble gas (hydrogen, 2).
{{< /caja >}}

**Metals** have few valence electrons and **lose** them (cations); **non-metals** have many and **gain** the ones they are missing (anions):

| Group | Family | Valence e⁻ | What does it do? | Ion |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Alkali metals | 1 | loses 1 | \(\ce{Na+}\), \(\ce{K+}\) |
| 2 | Alkaline earth metals | 2 | loses 2 | \(\ce{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\ce{Ca^{2+}}\) |
| 13 | Boron group | 3 | loses 3 | \(\ce{Al^{3+}}\) |
| 14 | Carbon group | 4 | usually shares | — |
| 15 | Nitrogen group | 5 | gains 3 | \(\ce{N^{3-}}\), \(\ce{P^{3-}}\) |
| 16 | Oxygen group | 6 | gains 2 | \(\ce{O^{2-}}\), \(\ce{S^{2-}}\) |
| 17 | Halogens | 7 | gains 1 | \(\ce{F-}\), \(\ce{Cl-}\) |
| 18 | Noble gases | 8 | nothing | do not form ions |

{{< caja cuidado >}}
the transition metals do not follow this table: many form ions with different charges, such as \(\ce{Fe^{2+}}\) and \(\ce{Fe^{3+}}\).
{{< /caja >}}

## 4. Chemical bonding

| Bond | Between… | What the electrons do | It forms | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ionic** | metal + non-metal | the metal **gives** them to the non-metal | ionic crystals | \(\ce{NaCl}\), \(\ce{MgO}\) |
| **Covalent** | non-metal + non-metal | they are **shared** | molecules or covalent crystals | \(\ce{H2O}\), \(\ce{CO2}\), diamond |
| **Metallic** | metal + metal | they **move freely** between the ions | metallic crystals | \(\ce{Fe}\), \(\ce{Cu}\), bronze |

| Substances… | Properties |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ionic** | solid, hard but brittle, high melting point; conduct electricity when molten or dissolved, not when solid. |
| **Molecular** | many are gases or liquids; low melting points; do not conduct. |
| **Metallic** | shiny, good conductors of heat and electricity, ductile and malleable. |

## 5. Towards chemical formulae

{{< caja idea "Chemical formula:" >}}
represents a substance with the **symbols** of its elements and **subscripts** that show how many atoms of each there are. The subscript 1 is not written: \(\ce{H2O}\) has 2 atoms of H and 1 of O.
{{< /caja >}}

- **Simple substances:** metals and noble gases are named like the element (\(\ce{Fe}\), iron). Molecular ones take a prefix: \(\ce{O2}\) dioxygen, \(\ce{O3}\) trioxygen (ozone), \(\ce{Cl2}\) dichlorine.
- **Cations:** element name + *ion*: \(\ce{Na+}\) sodium ion.
- **Anions:** root of the non-metal + **-ide**: \(\ce{Cl-}\) chloride, \(\ce{S^{2-}}\) sulfide, \(\ce{O^{2-}}\) oxide.

{{< figtexto lado="derecha" src="aspa.svg" alt="Crossing the charges: the 3+ charge of aluminium becomes the subscript of oxygen and the 2− charge of oxygen becomes the subscript of aluminium: Al2O3" ancho="190" >}}
**Formula of an ionic compound:** the charges must cancel out. The trick is to **cross the charges**: the number of each ion's charge becomes the subscript of the other one, and you simplify if you can (\(\ce{Mg2O2}\) → \(\ce{MgO}\)). The charges are not written in the formula.
{{< /figtexto >}}

{{< caja idea "Molecular mass:" >}}
the sum of the atomic masses of all the atoms in the formula (each mass times its subscript). In ionic compounds it is also called **formula mass**.
{{< /caja >}}

{{< formula >}}\ce{H2O}\!:\ 2 \cdot 1 + 16 = 18\ \text{u} \qquad \ce{CaCl2}\!:\ 40 + 2 \cdot 35.5 = 111\ \text{u}{{< /formula >}}
